Abstract
Sleeper screws are used to fasten the rails to the sleepers in the railway. Both high fatigue
strength and good corrosion properties are required. It is found that the screws lifetime often
is much shorter than expected in particular near switches and crossings, in curves and heavily
where traﬃc is heavy . In this thesis, a novel microstructural method has been established to
estimate the local stress for the corrosion fatigue crack initiation and propagation; an unique
corrosion fatigue chamber has been designed and fabricated not only for the present tension
corrosion fatigue but also for future compression and bending corrosion fatigue; and an accel-
erated corrosion fatigue has been tested to simulate the failure observed in a ﬁeld sleeper screw
and determine critical parameters.
A number of ﬂat and round dog-bone specimens were used in the tensile and fatigue tests.
The microstructure was characterized by Energy-Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy, Scanning Elec-
tron Microscopy, Electron Backscatter Diﬀraction and Transmission Electron Microscopy.
Then unused sleeper screws were received from Banedanmark, but no information was
given about their steel type. Therefore tensile tests were used to distinguish it among four
normal industrially available steels for sleeper screws. It was classiﬁed as type 35. . Low-
cycle fatigue tests have shown a similar dislocation cell microstructure and a similar cell size as
those at the failed surface of the broken ﬁeld sleeper screw after 69,721 cycles under 500 MPa,
whereas samples after 200,000 cycles, at 391MPa and 8,927 cycles, at 600 MPa did not develop
this microstructure. With the information and data from the low-cycle fatigue experiments, the
round dog-bone specimens were used for accelerated corrosion fatigue experiments to simulate
the real working environments of a sleeper screw. It is found that both the corrosion solution
and micro-current aﬀect the accelerated fatigue behavior.Compared with the 121,554 life cycles
for the normal corrosion fatigue at 500 MPa and 2 Hz, the accelerated corrosion fatigue leads
to failure at only 33, 960 cycles. The fracture topography of the latter sample shows similar
characteristics to those of the broken ﬁeld sleeper screw from Banedanmark.
It has been
demonstrated that the complex working conditions including the cyclic load, the stress level
and the corrosive environment contribute the ﬁnal failure of the screws.

1
Introduction
Railway is of signiﬁcant interest to reduce the burden of automobile and air travel in
congested corridors, and to reduce energy consumption and green house gas emission[1]. The
latest white paper on transport from the European Union suggests that railways are the key to
obtain a sustainable multi-modal inter-operable transport network[1]. Therefore it is important
to maintain the reliability, eﬃciency, and safety of railway systems and a lot of research has
been undertaken worldwide to improve the environment, safety and economic aspects of the
railway system.
Rails are fastened to the sleepers with railway screws, which are critical parts to ensure
the safety. In general, railways can serve for decades when properly maintained. However, the
screws often break after a few years. Especially in complex stress environments with eﬀects of
corrosion, the life of screws are often much shorter than the designed lifetime. This can cause
risks. In this project, a novel experimental method has been established to simulate the sleeper
screws condition and determine critical parameters.

2.1
Low cycle fatigue
Research on fatigue has been discussed for more than hundred years because of its practi-
cal importance. When it come to rail screw partfatigue failure of axles had become a common
problem in the 18th century. During the past decades, there were many aircraft accidents, due
to fatigue problems. These accidents made the fatigue damage of metallic materials under cyclic
stress a lot of topics. Then more and more researches and theories of fatigue cumulative damage
were developed[3]. For a conventional fatigue strength design, it is assumed that the material is
homogeneous and defect-free. The fatigue process is divided into three stages: crack formation,
crack propagation and ﬁnal fracture. In the 1960s, there were two important milestones. First
one is the Paris’ law, which suggests a crack growth equation that gives the rate of growth of
a fatigue crack as well as the stress intensity factor.[4] The second one is the Coﬃn-Manson
expression relating cycles to failure to plastic strain amplitude.[5]
Research on fatigue prob-
lems can often be divided into the following categories according to the number of cycles of
fatigue load action: super high cycle fatigue(more than 107), high cycle fatigue(105 - 107) and
low cycle fatigue(103 - 105).In this project, we focus on the low cycle fatigue characteristics of
the sleeper screws (500 - 200,000).
Ye D. et.al studyed the relation between the size of the dislocation cells and fatigue process.[6]
The dislocation structure inﬂuence the performance of the the metal products, and could be
ﬁner by plastic deformation to improve the performance.[7] R. Alain et.al studyed the the tem-
perature on the deformation mechanisms, the dislocation structures, the mechanical behavior
and damages processes, leading to important diﬀerences in fatigue lives. [8] The
2.2
Corrosion fatigue
During the life of a railway screw, it is suﬀering from diﬀerent forms of damage due to the in-
ﬂuence of the environment, including corrosion fatigue. The chemical degradation of materials,
corrosion, destroys the materials by chemical reactions with aggressive environments. [9] When
designing equipment or components, usually not only the mechanical properties, processing
performance and costs of the material should be considered, but also the requirements for the
corrosion mechanical stability. The corrosion mechanical stability is one of the most important
parameters that determine the life of the railway screws. [10]

Even though, it has been demonstrated that the amount of environmental enhancement of
crack growth rates in true corrosion fatigue cannot be accurately predicted from quantitative
models. [11] Ya.B.Unigovski found a reduction in the cyclic longevity as a result of the action
of corrosive environment[12]. The interactions among wear and corrosion could signiﬁcantly
increase total weight losses and reduction of either wear or corrosion could considerably decrease
the total weight loss[13].
The fracture characteristics of metal materials in corrosive environment are very diﬀerent
from those in general air environments, since metal materials are very sensitive to the corrosion
eﬀect and the strength reduces greatly.
The features of corrosion damage to steel products depend on many factors such as the
composition, the structure, the environment and local stress. [14, 16] Y.Shariﬁreviewed the
main aspects that are associated with stress concentration created by corrosion and developed a
function which could represent fatigue notch factor in terms of average corrosion penetration[17].
For most engineering structures and components operating under dynamic or alternating
loading conditions, a common notable character is that their service properties, especially
mechanical properties such as strength, stiﬀness, ductility, etc., deteriorates progressively with
increasing length of service[18]
There is a certain relationship between the forms of corrosion damage. For example,
inter-granular corrosion is developed by pit corrosion. [19] Various types of corrosion formed
by pits, holes, and other defects often lead to corrosion fatigue.
To sum up the impact on one speciﬁc material, the corrosive environment usually has the
following eﬀects on the mechanical properties:
• Corrosion causes continuous degradation of fatigue quality and shortens the life span;
• Combined eﬀects of service environment and residual tensile stress can cause corrosion
damage;
There are a lot of research concentrates on the alloy composition, crystal structure, heat
treatment or environmental eﬀects.[20, 21]

2.4
Stress corrosion crack
Stress corrosion is a form of damage that occurs in metallic materials under the combined
action of corrosive media and tensile or compressive stress. The accumulation of deformation
continues until the material’s ductility limit is exceeded. When the deformation exceeds the
fracture strain of the material, cracks appear. [25] When the stress corrosion of metals occurs,
the two factors of corrosion and stress promote each other. On the other hand, corrosion reduces
the eﬀective cross-sectional area of the metal and forms gaps on the surface, resulting in stress
concentration.

In this section, a detailed description of the experimental techniques and procedures used for
the characterization and mechanical testing of the sleeper screw specimens is given.
3.1
Screws used in this project
The screws have a two-phase microstructure consisting of ferrite and pearlite, as shown in
Fig.3.1. This is typical for medium-carbon steels and is a common choice for sleeper screws.
During the cooling process in the production of screws, proeutectoid ferrite grains precipitate
and grow from the austenite grain boundaries at ﬁrst, and then the lamellar pearlite with
alternating layered ferrite and cementite is formed by the decomposition of the remaining
eutectoid austenite.[26] The ferrite phase is soft and ductile, while the pearlite is hard and
reasonably ductile. As the carbon content increases, the volume fraction of pearlite increases
while that of ferrite decreases.

3.8
Electron backscatter diﬀraction
By electron backscatter diﬀraction (EBSD) crystallographic information from local areas of
the microstructure can be determined in a scanning electron microscope, including orientation
mapping and crystal type determination . This feature it a brilliant microstructural character-
ization tool . EBSD maps are formed by moving a focused probe of electrons point by point
across a grid of positions on the surface of a bulk sample in a SEM. At each point, some of
the electrons backscattered from the sample are collected by a detector to form an electron
backscatter diﬀraction pattern. [31] In the present study, EBSD is used to detect the dislo-
cation cell structure formation and to determine the dislocation cell size.
As mentioned in
the last sections, the focus of matrix microstructural characterization is on the dislocation cell
formation behavior and its size in the ferrite grains, with the aim to ﬁnd the appropriate stress
level and cycles to form a dislocation cell size, comparable to that (around 1 µm) at the failed
surface of the broken ﬁeld screw. So, the EBSD characterization of the 9 specimens (391 MPa
500 cycles, 5000 cycles and 200,000 cycles; 500 MPa 500 cycles, 5000 cycles and 69721 cycles;
600 MPa 500 cycles, 5000 cycles and 8,927 cycles) is selective and takes the following steps:
• EBSD characterization of the specimen at 391 MPa 200,000 cycles from three directions (X
represents the tension direction, Y represents the ﬂat/width plane normal and Z represents
the X×Y direction) to check if there is any diﬀerence. If the dislocation cell structure is
not clearly formed and its size is large than 1 m, the characterization of specimens at 391
MPa 500 and 5000 cycles will be dismissed; otherwise continue characterization;
• EBSD characterization of the specimen at 600 MPa 8,927 cycles. If the dislocation cell
structure is not clearly formed and its size is large than 1 µm, the characterization of
specimens at 600 MPa 500 and 5000 cycles will be dismissed; otherwise continue charac-
terization;

3.10
Energy Dispersive X-ray analysis of the failed fral screw
Energy Dispersive X-ray (EDX) microanalysis is introduced to detect the chemical compo-
sition of the corrosion products on the fracture surface and in the cracks of the failed sleeper
screws. This is to be able to select a proper solution for the present corrosion fatigue experi-
ments
EDX microanalysis is a technique of elemental analysis in a scanning electron microscope
based on the generation of characteristic X-rays that reveals the presence of elements present
in the specimens.[33]. When the ionized atoms return to their ground state, they will emit
characteristic X-rays and the energy is the potential energy resulting from the diﬀerence between
the two orbitals involved in the transition, which is thus characteristic of the element.[34]
According to theory, there are some limits related to EDX. Firstly, it does not separate ionic
and non-ionic species. Second, vacuum environment is required since the electron and X-rays
are strongly aﬀected by air molecules. Moreover, whereas X-ray detection is not aﬀected by
the chemical state of the elements, it is aﬀected by inter-elements interference, known as peak
overlap.

Summary
Normal and accelerated corrosion fatigue tests were carried out using round dog-bone speci-

conclusions are as follows.
• Cl and S elements were not detected by EDX and a 5 wt% NaCl was selected for the
present tests as conductivity is needed.
• A chamber to contain the corrosion solution and to mount the specimen was designed
and worked properly during the experiments.
• The fracture topography of the accelerated corrosion fatigue specimen is similar to the
ﬁeld screws, and many micro-cracks were observed.
• It is interesting that the macro strain level increase is similar in these two specimens
but the life cycle numbers are quite diﬀerent. This suggests that crack initiation and
propagation were faster due to the electrochemical reaction in the accelerated corrosion
fatigue specimen.

CONCLUSIONS
7
Conclusions
The type of the steel was distinguished by a simple tensile test to be of the 35 type. Flat
specimens were prepared from virgin screws and used in the subsequent low-cycle fatigue test.
It has been demonstrated that the state of the failed sleeper screw can be simulated by certain
stress level and cycles. The cycles vs strain curve show that with increasing number of cycles,
hardening and softening behavior occurs until the ﬁnal break.
By fracture topography analysis, three signiﬁcant crack areas were detected. Most of the
cracks originated at the sharp corners and more than one main crack initiation area exist.
The EBSD results of ﬂat dog-bone tests indicate the formation of dislocation cells and
their size are inﬂuenced by the stress level and number of cycles. It turned out to be possible to
reach the aim namely to ﬁnd the appropriate stress level and cycles, to form a similar dislocation
cell (around 1 µm) as in the failed ﬁeld sleeper screw. The best match was 500 MPa and 69,000
cycles.
The TEM images veriﬁed the results of EBSD showing clearly the dislocation cell structure
with cells of around 1 µm.
Based on these data and information, the further experiment – accelerated corrosion
fatigue experiment was optimized. One of the most critical parts of this project was to man-
ufacture a usable device for these experiments. The results was simple but practical chamber
design. Two samples were tested by normal and accelerated corrosion fatigue. They revealed
totally diﬀerent surface characteristics and diﬀerent number of cycles to break. Many inner
micro-cracks were detected on the fracture surface of the specimen tested with current, which
is similar to what is observed in the failed ﬁeld screw. It is interesting that the increase in
macro strain level is similar in these two specimens but the life time ( 121,554 and 33,560
cycles for the normal and accelerated corrosion fatigue tests, respectively) are quite diﬀerent.
This suggests that local crack initiation and propagation are faster due to the electrochemical
reaction in the accelerated corrosion fatigue specimen.

OUTLOOK
8
Outlook
The present project successfully realized the initial aims of the projects. However, there are
still many topics for the further research:
• The chamber could still be improved especially the concentricity error, if the specimen
could be machined smaller and the bottom cylinder part could be saved.
• The S-N (stress - life cycles) curves for normal and accelerated tension corrosion fatigue
tests, should be compared with the dry tension fatigue test.
• The relation between the surface intrusion/extrusion, surface roughness, crack initiation
and propagation, fatigue stresses and the life cycles of the screw material could be inves-
tigated.
• A comparative study of the failed mechanisms of the above three types of fatigue tests.
• Use the knowledge from more lab experiments to prolong the life of ﬁeld screws.
